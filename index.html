  • Solar EV Rover: Accounts for Mars atmospheric constraints (no free oxygen for combustion engines) and signal delays.

🛰️ NASA Telemetry Sources

  • Mars Global Localization: Onboard terrain-matching navigation allowing rovers like Perseverance to self-locate without GPS.
  • NASA DONKI: A real NASA space-weather database that can inform solar-event research. This prototype does not fetch live DONKI data.
  • OSDR & NTRS: Astronaut health monitoring and biological research parameters.
  • Mineral research: NASA's Curiosity CheMin instrument has identified Mars minerals including olivine, pyroxene, hematite, magnetite, gypsum, jarosite, and tridymite. Other collection entries are included as educational mineral types; their exact in-game locations are fictional.
  • Mars environment: Average temperature is about −59°C; surface pressure is roughly 600 Pa; gravity is about 3.71 m/s². Mars has no present-day global magnetic field, though ancient localized crustal magnetic regions remain.
  • Weather research: NASA's Perseverance MEDA instrument measures wind, temperature, humidity, and dust. This game uses a simplified simulation, not a live feed.
  • Data note: Mineral names and science facts are based on NASA mission references. The map, deposit coordinates, crew activities, and day/night clock are game systems, not real Mars telemetry.
  • NASA: Curiosity CheMin mineral analysis

🚀 PERSEVERANCE // REAL NASA ROVER MISSION

I'm a simulation rover inspired by NASA's Perseverance. Here's the story of the real robot that traveled from Earth and landed on Mars.

🎯 What was its mission?
Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater on 18 February 2021. It studies ancient environments, searches for signs that microbial life may once have existed, collects and stores rock and soil samples, and tests technologies that can help prepare for future human exploration. Its companion helicopter, Ingenuity, demonstrated powered flight on another planet.
🛰️ How did it get to Mars?
It launched from Earth on 30 July 2020 aboard an Atlas V rocket. After a journey of about seven months, it reached Mars. During the final seven minutes, its heat shield protected it during atmospheric entry, a parachute slowed it down, and a rocket-powered sky crane lowered the rover gently onto the surface. This sequence is called Entry, Descent and Landing (EDL).
🧪 What does it investigate?
It uses cameras, spectrometers, weather sensors and a drill to study rocks, dust and the Martian environment. It stores selected samples in sealed tubes for possible future return to Earth.
